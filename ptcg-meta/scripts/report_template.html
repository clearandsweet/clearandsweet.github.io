H Rotation Meta Ledger

What "rotation" means here

Every card whose only legal printings carry the H mark leaves Standard. Cards with an I or J print stay. K cards have not been revealed beyond three Aura Seeker (M7) cards: Mega Lucario Z ex, Sigilyph ex and Riolu. No date is announced; the pattern points to late January 2027 in Japan and roughly April 2027 internationally. I checked all 289 Japanese Mega-era reprints of rotating cards, including the "Battle Collection" starter reprints. Every one is still H, so nothing that rotates has an I/J lifeline yet.

The current window is the four 2027-season Regionals (Baltimore, Brisbane, Frankfurt, Recife). Day 2 lift is an archetype's Day 2 rate divided by the field's. Reading this

  • The top deck by a wide margin, Dragapult ex, is H-only, along with Dreepy, Drakloak and every Dusknoir variant partner.
  • The universal engine package (Fezandipiti ex, Budew, Munkidori, Buddy-Buddy Poffin, Night Stretcher, Crispin, Unfair Stamp) rotates together, so even "Intact" archetypes lose consistency cards.
  • Teal Mask Ogerpon ex rotates, which takes the Basic Box and Ogerpon Grass engines with it.
  • Decks built on I/J Megas (Kangaskhan, Excadrill, Lucario, Lopunny, Greninja, Starmie) and N's Zoroark keep their attackers.
  • Every ACE SPEC in Standard is H. After rotation the ACE SPEC slot is empty unless K adds new ones. The regression values the best of them (Secret Box, Unfair Stamp) at about +3 match-win points over an average card.

    05 · Support Pokémon play-down

    How often support Pokémon hit the board

    For each list that runs the card, a Monte Carlo of 600 games deals a 7-card hand (mulligans included), sets 6 Prizes, then lets the player see draws plus extra digging from Supporters and Abilities, and use searchers such as Ultra Ball, Poffin, Poké Pad, Cyrano and Dawn. A searcher is assumed free for this card only part of the time, since it has other jobs. That gives the chance the card is reachable by the turn it matters. A desire factor (would you bench it?) is then applied against each opponent archetype, weighted by meta share. Desire is the judgment call I most want you to check.

    06 · The Advantage stat

    Card-equivalents per game

    Definition

    One unit, a card-equivalent (CE), is the value of drawing one random card. Every benefit converts into it: search = 1.5 CE, look-2-keep-1 = 1.25, an extra Energy attachment = 1 CE of tempo (+1 if the Energy didn't come from your hand), each retreat Energy saved = 2 CE, each card discarded as a cost = −0.5. Damage and healing convert through Prizes: HP of the current meta ≈ 1 Prize ≈ 3 CE. Item lock is valued as the opponent's held-back Items plus a setup-tempo penalty.

    Advantage per game = expected uses × CE per use. Expected uses come from the same simulation: when the card is reachable, how many copies are in play each turn (evolution lag, bench caps, survival), the chance the game is still going, and how often the trigger is live (Grass in hand for Teal Dance, a KO last turn for Flip the Script…).

    07b · Calibration from results

    What the results say each resource is worth

    Every list from all 14 events ( lists with 3+ recorded games) was regressed on its contents. Match win rate (ties = ⅓) and Day 2 were both used as outcomes. Controls are archetype × format and event fixed effects, plus a pilot-skill term: each player's record relative to their deck at their other events, available for of entries. Standard errors are clustered by player.

    How the weights are fit

    • Stage 1: the win-rate and Day 2 change per extra copy of each common card, within its archetype. Evolution lines that always move together are merged.
    • Stage 2: those per-copy effects are explained by what one more copy adds. For Items that's its draw, search, acceleration and so on, with mild diminishing returns. For Supporters it's the same minus the Supporter it displaces, because you play one per turn. For engines it's the simulated marginal uses of the last copy.
    • Search, the best-measured resource, anchors the scale at 1.5 CE per card. A prior centred on my hand-set weights (±1 CE) keeps thinly identified components (look-and-pick, item lock, evolution tempo) from swinging wildly. The "data only" column drops the prior.
    • Marginal is not average. These weights price the next copy at the counts people actually play, so they come out lower for resources decks already have plenty of.

    08 · Damage value

    Prize progress per attack against the current field

    Each attacker's typical damage is run against a defender pool built from every archetype's real attackers, weighted by share, with Weakness and Resistance applied. Lillie's Clefairy ex gets Fairy Zone against Dragons. Prizes per attack = Σ share × min(1, damage ÷ HP) × Prizes the defender gives, plus bench damage at a discount. Prize efficiency divides that by the attacker's own Prize liability.

    11 · Where to take this next

    Ways to calculate, arrange and use the data

    Skill-adjusted deck strength

    Fit a Bradley–Terry / logistic model on all recorded matches with a term for each player and each archetype. Players recur across events, so this separates "good deck" from "good pilots". The output is a deck-strength number that won't be fooled by one strong team piloting a rogue list.

    Matchup engine and meta forecasting

    Turn the pairings into a shrunk matchup matrix, compute expected win rate against any assumed field, then run replicator dynamics or a Nash solver to see where the meta drifts. Re-run on the survivor set (plus K decks as they appear) for a post-rotation forecast.

    Rotation-loss in CE, not card counts

    Strip H cards from every list, refill each slot with the best legal replacement from the replacement map, then re-score with the Advantage model. The drop in CE per game is a sharper "how hurt is this deck" number than counting cards.

    Fit the exchange rates to results

    I set the CE weights by hand. Regress Day 2 conversion (or match win rate) on each list's total CE by component (draw, search, accel, retreat, lock) and let the data set the weights. If Teal Dance CE predicts wins better than Recon Directive CE, the weights should show it.

    Card-level effects within an archetype

    Inside one archetype, regress Day 2 on card counts (2 Budew vs 1, Latias vs none). Classify cards as core (≥90% inclusion, stable count), flex, or tech, and report each tech's lift with intervals. This turns "list optimization" into numbers.

    Calibrate play-down with real logs

    TCG Live battle logs (your own, or exports like Trainer Hill's battle journal) record which Pokémon were benched on which turn. A few hundred logs would replace my dig-rate and desire guesses with measured rates per archetype and matchup.

    Recency, region and Day 2 weighting

    Weight events with a half-life (newer counts more), split NA / EU / LATAM / OCE, and offer a "quality share" weighted to Day 2. These views differ a lot here: Japan's City League field is already a different shape from the international one.

    K-block drop-in

    Card text is the only input the replacement finder and Advantage model need. As Aura Seeker and later K cards are revealed, add their JSON and they get scored against today's staples automatically, e.g. Mega Lucario Z ex's Supporter search vs Meowth ex.

    Arrangement: deck pages and card pages

    Every number here can be pivoted two ways: one page per archetype (share trend, conversion, matchups, rotation loss, key cards, replacements) and one page per card (usage trend, by archetype, Day 2 lift, legality, CE). That is how this turns into a dashboard you keep using.

    Uncertainty everywhere

    Shares and conversions carry Wilson intervals, win rates carry standard errors, and the simulations can bootstrap lists. Showing a band on every number keeps a 9-player deck with a 3/9 Day 2 rate from reading like a top-tier result.

    13 · Data notes

    Sources, coverage and caveats

      • Archetype labels are Limitless Labs' own. "Other" groups rogue lists.
      • Top cut flags cover few players (top 8 to 16 per event), so top-cut columns are directional only.
      • Labs standings are computed outside the official software and can contain small errors. A few players have no published list.
      • Trainer Hill's multi-deck matchup endpoint returned server errors, so matchups come from Labs pairings. Trainer Hill's archetype counts (17,292 decks from in-person and online events with 50+ players since 17 Sep, all divisions) were used as a cross-check. They show the same top six archetypes, with Dragapult at 12.1% there vs 17.4% at Masters Regionals.
      • The Advantage and play-down numbers are model outputs. The CE weights are now partly fitted to results (section 07b), but the uses-per-game side is still simulated.
      • Battle logs: Trainer Hill publishes no battle-log data. Its Battle Journal (free, stored in your browser) and Battle Journal+ (Patreon, login) are personal match trackers. They record deck, opponent, result, turn order and tags, not turn-by-turn logs, and nothing is shared publicly. TCG Live's own Battle Log text is the right source. scripts/tcgl_logs.py parses it (first turn each Pokémon hits the board, Abilities used, attacks, Items per turn, item-locked turns) and outputs the same quantities the simulations predict. Drop exported logs in data/logs/ to calibrate.
